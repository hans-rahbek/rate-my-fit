    The maths, without the mystery

    Fisher LDA finds a projection direction that separates the sample means relative to within-group scatter. The dashed boundary is perpendicular to that direction and passes midway between projected means. A small isotropic ridge, λ = 0.02 × total observation weight, is added to the scatter matrix. This defines a stable solution even with only one point in each group; the game uses regularised Fisher LDA throughout.

    We compare two fits: squared label errors and regularised Fisher J. For squared errors, labels −1 and +1 are predicted from signed distance to your line using its best nonnegative least-squares gain. SSE sums weighted squared errors, MSE = SSE / total weight, and label R² = 1 − SSE / total weight (relative to zero-label prediction). Relative label-fit error = (your SSE − reference SSE) / (total weight − reference SSE). Relative Fisher error = (reference J − your J) / reference J. Each error is clipped to 0–100%. Statistical fit = 100% minus the average of these two errors. Final fit is the lower of statistical fit and boundary precision. Boundary precision is a game metric, not a statistical goodness-of-fit test. Its error is population-sensitive pressure × max(sin²(angle difference), (midpoint offset / (2 × regularised within-group SD))²), clipped to 0–100%. Small samples receive explicit assistance: t = clamp((population − 2) / 78, 0, 1), maturity m = t²(3 − 2t), adjusted statistical error = raw error × (0.35 + 0.65m), and precision pressure = (0.4 + 0.6m) × (1 + 0.12 × max(0, round − 5) × m). At 80 observations, raw statistical errors regain full sensitivity; later-round pressure grows gradually. Midpoint offset is the perpendicular distance from the sample-means midpoint to your line; regularised SD is sqrt((reference projected within-group SS + ridge) / total weight). Reversing a line’s normal leaves its precision unchanged. Both components must reach 95% to pass. SSE, MSE, and R² count once. Within-group squared spread is diagnostic. With ridge regularisation, LDA maximises regularised J; it need not minimise unregularised label SSE.

    Overall score is the largest population passed in a run. Population follows N_next = 2 × ceil((N + 2) × (1 + (g − 1) × retained bonus) × min(1.50, 1 + 0.15 × log₂(remaining risk multiplier)) × analysis multiplier / 2), with a minimum next population of N + 2; retained bonus starts at 1 each round and is multiplied by 0.9 per cull and 0.8^rank per denoise or separator use; g = 1.12 + min(0.18, 0.005 × (round − 1)). Resamples never grow population. Spread, proximity and contamination ranks scale automatically on a repeating three-round cycle. Contamination strength is zero through 40 points and full at 120: with t = clamp((N − 40) / 80, 0, 1), strength = t²(3 − 2t), scaling probability and displacement. There are no run skill points or spending choices. Three ranked permanent intervention perks can edit a dataset before submission: culling removes the Euclidean-farthest point from each group per use, allowing 1–5 pairs per round and keeping at least two points per group; denoising contracts within-group deviations by factors 0.85, 0.70 or 0.55; separation translates group centres apart by factors 1.20, 1.40 or 1.60 without changing within-group deviations. Denoising and separation each have one use per round. Resamples do not refill intervention charges. LDA is recalculated on the edited data. Removed points are excluded from cleared population, future population growth and research. These are game interventions rather than statistical data-cleaning recommendations.

    Control the Chaos research becomes available after successfully clearing 100 population; Dubious Methods research after 1,000. These milestones stay unlocked across runs. Individual perks still require research credits. Research uses lifetime passed population P. Base research potential is (sqrt(1 + 0.8P) − 1) / 2, with integer thresholds at cumulative totals 5n(n + 1); each passed plot adds its full population, including replays. Failed rounds and Dev do not add research. Research progress earned by each passed plot is the increase in base research potential, multiplied by 2^((fit − 95) / 5), then by the remaining risk multiplier. The exact fit bonus rises from ×1 at 95% to ×2 at 100%. Progress accumulates with the saved fractional balance; only whole credits are paid out. Lifetime population remains actual submitted dots. Research and permanent perks survive failed runs. The leaderboard starts with 10 built-in benchmark runs and keeps the 10 best entries; new completed runs need at least one passed population, ordered by maximum passed population, average fit, maximum fit, then earlier run date. Average fit includes the final failed submission. A three-letter handle is requested only at the end of a qualifying normal run. Earlier completed scores are preserved where they qualify, with unnamed entries shown as —. Scores are saved on this browser. Dubious-method perks modify the observed data before submission. Selective weighting uses frequency weights (2×, 3× or 4×); weighted means, pooled scatter, ridge, label-fit statistics, contours, compass and density estimates all use those weights. Actual-dot classification accuracy and population remain unweighted. Winsorisation caps radial distances by group quantile. Label reassignment swaps one point per class. Axis compression acts around the midpoint of the two weighted group centres. Cherry-picking retains the same number per class, trimming excess by distance to the retained class centre. Invalid/no-op edits cost no charge. Every accepted intervention is listed in the round’s Methods receipt.

    How risk works

    Start each run with 4 tokens. Each passed round earns 2 tokens, or 3 at 99% fit or better, up to 12 banked. Planning rolls one offer per category: dispersion, overlap and contamination. Each offer independently rolls its data effect and reward bonus (8–24%). Choose exactly one for 2 tokens before starting the next round. The values stay fixed while you compare, browse Research or revisit results. All purchased effects accumulate for the rest of the run, with no category purchase caps. Spread and centre-distance factors compound; contamination affects a fraction of remaining clean points and increases tail strength. Reward percentages add to the running risk multiplier. Contamination requires a forecast population of 40.

    Reward percentages add to the run’s risk multiplier rather than compounding: two +15% offers give ×1.30. Population growth gets a smaller bonus: 1 + 0.15 × log₂(risk multiplier), capped at ×1.50. Chaos tools cancel corresponding reward bonuses for that round; Dubious Methods cancel the risk reward bonus. Purchases never change the completed plot or its research reward.
